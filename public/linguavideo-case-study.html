02 / Problem Statement

Localisation teams operating in fragmented workflows

Organisations producing multilingual video content — from e-learning platforms to corporate training providers — were managing localisation across 3 to 6 disconnected tools: subtitle editors, external translation services, voice-over studios, manual QA spreadsheets, and no unified task tracking. Every handoff was a potential failure point.

Translators had no access to shared glossaries or style context, so terminology drifted across videos. Project managers had no visibility into who was working on what — resulting in missed deadlines, duplicate work, and inconsistent localised output that damaged brand credibility in target markets.

"By the time a Hindi translation reaches the voice-over stage, the terminology has already changed twice. There's no single glossary everyone uses."

— Content Localisation Lead, Discovery Interview

"I have to email four people just to find out which subtitle file is the final approved version for a video."

— Video Project Manager, Discovery Interview

🔍 Discovery (Weeks 1–3)

  • 9 stakeholder interviews — translators, PMs, content owners, VO artists
  • Contextual inquiry — shadowed active localisation sessions end-to-end
  • Current-state workflow mapping across 3 enterprise content teams
  • Competitive audit: Amara, Kapwing, Rev, Smartcat, Lokalise
  • Terminology & glossary management pain cluster analysis

🧭 Define (Week 4)

  • Affinity mapping → 4 core pain clusters identified
  • 4 user personas: Org Owner, Translator, Project Manager, VO Artist
  • Jobs-to-be-done per role — upload, assign, translate, review, publish
  • HMW statements prioritised with PM + engineering leads

💡 Ideation (Weeks 5–6)

  • Crazy-8 sketching workshops (2 sessions, 6 participants)
  • Information architecture workshops across content ops and eng
  • In-context translation panel concept exploration
  • Glossary & language-pair management flow design sprints

🔄 Prototype + Validate (Weeks 7–12)

  • 2 rounds of lo-fi wireframe testing (5 participants each)
  • Interactive Figma prototype validated via Maze
  • Hi-fi pilot with 2 content teams across 3 weeks
  • Metrics: task assignment speed, subtitle review accuracy, time-on-task
04 / User Flow & Information Architecture

From raw video to published multilingual content

The core IA decision was an organisation-centric workspace — each organisation manages its own video library, task assignments, member roles, and glossary, while a shared review pipeline ensures quality control at every stage. The platform intercepts at each step, providing in-context subtitle editing, inline translation review, and one-click task assignment.

06 / Hi-fi Designs

Final screens, production-ready

The light-theme design was chosen to keep subtitle text and media content front and centre — where readability and long-session clarity matter most. Indigo brand elements signal navigation and interactive actions, while semantic status colours (green for completed, amber for in-progress, red for failed) remain instantly distinguishable across all task states. The persistent translation panel keeps context visible without interrupting the editor workflow.
