02 / Problem Statement

Knowledge graphs built in fragmented toolchains

Enterprise teams building ontologies — from e-commerce product catalogs to healthcare entities and supply chain graphs — were operating across 5 to 8 disconnected tools: Protégé for authoring, GitHub for versioning, Excel for mapping, custom scripts for validation, and manual handoffs to engineering. There was no unified environment.

Data architects and ontology engineers had no shared workspace. Critical decisions about class definitions, property mappings, and import dependencies were lost in Slack threads, emails, and outdated wiki pages — creating inconsistent, unreliable knowledge models that broke downstream AI pipelines.

"I spend two hours every morning reconciling who changed what in the ontology. There's no history, no review process — it's just chaos."

— Senior Ontology Engineer, Discovery Interview

"We have 7 ontologies across 4 teams and no one knows which version is actually deployed in production."

— Data Architect, Discovery Interview

No Unified Authoring Environment

How Might We

How might we design a unified ontology workspace that gives every team role-relevant control — so knowledge engineers can author, validate, and publish structured graphs collaboratively, without the chaos of disconnected toolchains?

🔍 Discovery (Weeks 1–3)

  • 11 stakeholder interviews — engineers, architects, PMs, data scientists
  • Contextual inquiry — shadowed ontology authoring sessions
  • Current-state toolchain mapping across 4 enterprise teams
  • Competitive audit: Protégé, TopBraid, Stardog, PoolParty
  • Data governance & schema review with engineering leads

🧭 Define (Week 4)

  • Affinity mapping → 5 core pain clusters
  • 4 user personas: Ontology Engineer, Data Architect, Product Manager, Admin
  • Jobs-to-be-done per role — author, validate, publish, reuse
  • HMW statements prioritised with PM + architecture leads

💡 Ideation (Weeks 5–6)

  • Crazy-8 sketching workshops (2 sessions, 8 participants)
  • Information architecture workshops with eng + data ops
  • AI-assisted draft generation opportunity mapping
  • Registry & import flow concept exploration

🔄 Prototype + Validate (Weeks 7–12)

  • 2 rounds of lo-fi wireframe testing (5 participants each)
  • Interactive Figma prototype validated via Maze
  • Hi-fi pilot with 3 enterprise teams across 2 weeks
  • Metrics: task completion rate, error discovery speed, time-on-task
04 / User Flow & Information Architecture

From raw schema to published knowledge graph

The core IA decision was a project-centric workspace — each ontology lives in an isolated project context with its own classes, data sources, mappings, and validation history, while a shared registry enables cross-project reuse. The platform intercepts at every stage, providing real-time validation, AI-assisted drafting, and one-click registry import.

06 / Hi-fi Designs

Final screens, production-ready

The dark-theme enterprise design was chosen for data-dense environments where engineers work in extended sessions — indigo brand signals structure and intelligence, while semantic status colors remain instantly distinguishable across all screen conditions. Role-based modules reduce context switching while the persistent job panel keeps background tasks visible without interrupting the authoring flow.
