02 / Problem Statement

Regional language audio stuck in fragmented workflows

Content creators, educators, and small businesses producing audio in Indian regional languages — Hindi, Marathi, Bengali, Gujarati, Punjabi — were operating across 4 to 6 disconnected tools: translation apps, text-to-speech converters, recording studios, cloud storage, and manual review processes. There was no unified platform.

Users had no way to manage voice preferences, language-specific glossaries, or production history in one place. Critical context about translation choices and voice styles was lost across email threads and browser tabs — creating inconsistent, low-quality audio outputs that broke audience trust.

"I spend hours every week converting, uploading, checking — just to get one Hindi podcast episode ready. There's no one place to do all of this."

— Regional Content Creator, Discovery Interview

"We want to reach Marathi and Bengali audiences but the translation quality is always off — we have no way to customise words to our brand voice."

— Education Platform Lead, Discovery Interview
🔀

No Unified Audio Generation Workspace

Users juggled multiple tools with no single dashboard — leading to lost project history, inconsistent voice settings, and wasted re-work on every new file.

4+ toolsManual handoffs
🌐

Generic Translations Break Context

Standard translation APIs failed on brand-specific terms and regional idioms. No glossary system meant every regional project required manual correction post-generation.

No custom vocabWrong transliterations
🎙️

Voice Inconsistency Across Projects

Without voice cloning, users couldn't maintain a consistent brand voice across episodes. Generic TTS voices felt robotic and impersonal, reducing listener engagement significantly.

No voice memoryNo personalisation
📊

Zero Feedback & Quality Loop

Teams had no structured way to rate or comment on generated audio. Quality issues were discovered only after distribution — with no in-platform mechanism to flag problems or track reviewer ratings.

No rating systemNo review trail
How Might We

How might we design a unified voice generation platform that gives every creator control over language, voice, and quality — so regional content teams can produce, review, and distribute multilingual audio without the chaos of disconnected tools?

🔍 Discovery (Weeks 1–3)

  • 9 stakeholder interviews — creators, educators, marketers, admins
  • Contextual inquiry — shadowed multilingual content production sessions
  • Current-state toolchain mapping across 3 regional content teams
  • Competitive audit: Murf, ElevenLabs, Suno, Speechify, Koo
  • Language & regional nuance review with linguistic consultants

🧭 Define (Week 4)

  • Affinity mapping → 4 core pain clusters
  • 4 user personas: Creator, Educator, Brand Marketer, Platform Admin
  • Jobs-to-be-done per role — generate, translate, clone, review
  • HMW statements prioritised with PM + engineering leads

💡 Ideation (Weeks 5–6)

  • Crazy-8 sketching workshops (2 sessions, 6 participants)
  • Information architecture workshops with content ops team
  • Voice cloning UX opportunity mapping
  • Glossary & custom vocabulary concept exploration

🔄 Prototype + Validate (Weeks 7–12)

  • 2 rounds of lo-fi wireframe testing (4 participants each)
  • Interactive Figma prototype validated via Maze
  • Hi-fi pilot with 2 regional content teams across 2 weeks
  • Metrics: task completion rate, audio satisfaction score, time-on-task
04 / User Flow & Information Architecture

From uploaded document to distributed audio

The core IA decision was a project-centric workspace — each audio generation lives in an isolated project context with its own source file, language settings, voice profile, and feedback history, while a shared glossary enables cross-project terminology consistency. The platform intercepts at every stage, providing real-time configuration options, voice cloning, and one-click audio download.

05 / Lo-fi Wireframes

From rough sketches to structured wireframes

Two rounds of lo-fi testing with 4 regional content creators each. Key learnings: users needed immediate upload confirmation feedback rather than a silent progress state, the glossary needed a bilingual side-panel so users could configure terms while seeing translations, and the feedback table needed star ratings glanceable without opening each row.

06 / Hi-fi Designs

Final screens, production-ready

The dark navy header with clean white content panels was chosen for audio-focused workflows where creators context-switch between configuration and playback — indigo brand signals intelligence and structure, while green upload confirmation and amber processing states remain instantly distinguishable during rapid production cycles. Role-based modules reduce cognitive load while the persistent audio player keeps preview accessible without interrupting the configuration flow.
