02 / Problem Statement

The fragmented referral journey costing lives & revenue

Home infusion therapy referrals involve up to 7 distinct teams — clinical, financial, pharmacy, scheduling, transport, intake, and nursing — each operating in siloed, disconnected systems. The result was a chaotic, error-prone patient journey with no single source of truth.

Coordinators were simultaneously juggling spreadsheets, fax machines, phone calls, and 3–4 legacy portals. Critical information — auth numbers, insurance cards, clinical flags — was frequently missing, lost, or duplicated across systems.

"I spend 40% of my day just chasing down a piece of paper or an auth number that should already be in the system."

— Financial Coordinator, Discovery Interview

"By the time I find out there's a problem, the patient has already missed their first infusion dose."

— Clinical Reviewer, Discovery Interview
⏱️

Turnaround Time Crisis

Average TAT was 22+ hours per referral due to manual handoffs, repeated data entry, and zero real-time status visibility across teams.

TAT: 22h avgTarget: 8h
🔒

No Unified Workspace

Five separate legacy tools handled different referral stages. No shared patient record meant each team re-entered the same data, repeatedly.

5 tools4× data re-entry
📄

Document Chaos

Faxed referrals arrived without MRN or patient context. Staff manually matched orphan documents — 15–30 min per document, every day.

Orphan docs dailyManual matching
📉

Invisible Bottlenecks

Supervisors had zero real-time visibility into queue depth, SLA compliance, or exactly where referrals were stalling.

No SLA trackingReactive only
How Might We

How might we design a unified, automation-first referral platform that gives every team role-relevant visibility and dramatically reduces manual effort — so clinicians can focus on patient care, not paperwork?

🔍 Discovery (Weeks 1–3)

  • 14 stakeholder interviews across 5 user roles
  • Contextual inquiry — shadowed coordinators for 3 days
  • Workflow mapping of current-state referral journey
  • Competitive audit of 6 healthcare SaaS platforms
  • HIPAA compliance review with legal team

🧭 Define (Week 4)

  • Affinity mapping → 6 core pain themes
  • 5 user personas (Financial, Clinical, Acceptance, Scheduling, Supervisor)
  • Jobs-to-be-done framework per role
  • HMW statements prioritised with PM + clinical leads

💡 Ideation (Weeks 5–6)

  • Crazy-8 sketching sessions (3 workshops)
  • Information architecture workshops with eng + ops
  • AI/automation opportunity mapping
  • Role-based workspace concept exploration

🔄 Prototype + Validate (Weeks 7–12)

  • 2 rounds of lo-fi paper prototyping (6 participants each)
  • Interactive Figma prototype tested via Maze
  • Hi-fi pilot with 4 live coordinators over 1 week
  • Usability metrics: task completion, error rate, time-on-task
04 / User Flow & Information Architecture

End-to-end patient journey, mapped

The core IA decision was role-based workspace separation — each user lands in a tailored queue, yet the underlying patient record is unified. This reduces cognitive load while maintaining a single source of truth. Automation intercepts at every stage, handling 94% of routine tasks without coordinator intervention.
